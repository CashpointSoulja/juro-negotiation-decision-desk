# Jobs to be done

**Core job.** When a counterparty sends back a marked-up contract, I want to know which changes my playbook already allows and which need me or an approver, so that I can turn the draft around quickly without accepting something my team would not.

| Who | Job | What gets in the way today (hypothesis) | What the desk does |
|---|---|---|---|
| Commercial counsel (reviewer) | Clear the in-playbook clauses fast and spend attention on the real exceptions | A suggestion without a reason means re-reading the clause and the playbook anyway | Cites the rule, the allowed deviation and the approver next to the wording |
| Reviewer | Not be the person who waved through an uncapped indemnity | Confident-looking output on wording the system did not really read | Blocks and says why: missing rule, conflicting rules or unreadable term |
| Playbook owner (GC, legal ops) | Keep the playbook complete and consistent | Gaps and conflicts surface only when a deal goes wrong | Each block names the gap (e.g. "Quayside has no governing-law rule"), and evals track it |
| Deal desk or sales | Know what approval a deal needs, and from whom | Approvals are discovered late | Deviation results name the approver (Commercial Counsel, DPO, Legal Ops) |
| Head of legal | Show that automation is safe before scaling it | No measured error rates | Eval suite with explicit FP/FN and a release gate against a baseline |

**Emotional job:** "I can defend this decision later." The audit trail and memo exist for that.
